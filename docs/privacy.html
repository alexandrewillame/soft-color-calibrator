Privacy policy

Soft Color Calibrator (Windows) and SoftCal Camera (Android, iPhone and iPad) · Effective 3 November 2026

Soft Color Calibrator and its companion app for Android, iPhone and iPad, SoftCal Camera, are free, open-source apps made by Alexandre Willame (“I” below). This policy explains what the apps do with your data. The short version: they do not send me, or anyone else, anything.

  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party code that collects data.
  • I run no servers and never receive any data from the apps.
  • SoftCal Camera sends its camera measurements only to Soft Color Calibrator on your own PC, over your local network, while you use them together.
  • Soft Color Calibrator keeps its files on your PC.

SoftCal Camera (Android, iPhone and iPad)

The camera

SoftCal Camera uses your phone's back camera only to measure your computer's screen, and only while Soft Color Calibrator on your PC asks for it: the camera turns on when the PC asks for a picture or a measurement, and off 10 seconds after the last request. The app never saves pictures or videos on your phone, and keeps no history.

What it sends, and to whom

It sends data only to the copy of Soft Color Calibrator that you pair it with by typing the 6-digit code the phone shows, on your local network. The phone's screen always says what it is sending and how often.

None of this goes to the internet, to me, or to any third party. When the connection ends, the phone puts its camera back to automatic and forgets the session.

Being found on your network

While it is open, the app announces itself on your local network under your phone's model name (the way printers and speakers do, with DNS-SD, called Bonjour on Apple devices), so that your PC can list it. Other devices on the same network can see that announcement. It stops when you close the app (on an iPhone or iPad, as soon as the app leaves the screen). On an iPhone or iPad, iOS first asks your permission for the local network.

Security

The 6-digit code never travels over the network: each side proves it knows it, and after 5 wrong attempts the phone shows a new one. Only one PC can be connected at a time. The connection itself is not encrypted: it carries measurement numbers and gray pictures of your screen. Use the apps on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi, rather than public Wi-Fi.

On iPhone and iPad, the app's App Store page shows "Data Not Collected", and the privacy manifest inside the app says the same.

The app has two links, which open in your browser only when you tap them: “Privacy policy” opens this page, and the address of the project on GitHub (shown until your PC connects) opens the page where Soft Color Calibrator for Windows is downloaded. The app itself sends nothing to either site.

Soft Color Calibrator (Windows)

Soft Color Calibrator keeps everything it makes on your PC, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\SoftColorCalibrator: measurements, reports, your monitor profiles and settings, calibration files and logs. These include your monitors' names and model identifiers (read from the monitors themselves) and the name of the camera or phone that measured. It never uploads them, has no telemetry, and does not check for updates online.

  • Webcam: used only in the camera and measuring steps; pictures are analysed in memory and never saved.
  • Phone: the PC connects to the phone you choose; it does not accept incoming connections.
  • Web search: if you click “Look it up” next to Panel type, your browser opens a web search (Google Search) for your monitor's model. That search is covered by the search engine's own privacy policy.

Children

The apps are not directed at children, and they collect no personal information from anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

I hold no data about you, so there is nothing to request or delete on my side. Everything stays on your devices, under your control: uninstall SoftCal Camera to remove it from your phone; on your PC, remove any installed calibration from within the app, delete its folder, and delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\SoftColorCalibrator.

Check it yourself

Both apps are open source: everything described here can be verified in the source code.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date, and the release notes will say so.
